## Output Rules

- Writing should feel authored, not generated.
- Match the register to the surface. Casual capture differs from published output.
- No filler language by default.
- When in doubt, be specific rather than general.
- Prefer concrete over abstract.
- Derived copy answers to the shipped artifact. Social posts, email versions, explainers and any copy reused from a published piece are drawn from the shipped piece, never from a draft or the session that produced it. A word the Sovereign cut stays cut downstream. Grep every provenance claim (a quoted phrase attributed to a source) against the file it cites before saving.
- Accuracy to the lived detail. Where a draft rounds a lived fact, put back what actually happened. When a draft states a time, a count, a duration or a superlative from the Sovereign's telling, render it as they lived it. Keep their "-ish" and their hedge on any claim they cannot verify. Never round for rhythm. The correction can run either way: a detail the Sovereign asks to soften stays soft. The rule is accuracy rather than maximum precision.
- A house term used as a metaphor takes quotation marks at its first appearance in each piece a reader meets on its own (each article, each social post, each slide set with its caption). After that it runs bare unless the quotes help again. A reader may see only one piece, so it cannot lean on the quotes in the one before. Derived copy re-applies this per piece instead of inheriting quotes from its source sentence.
- People are shown, never sorted. Copy describes a person or a reader through examples of what they do and live, never with a type label ("the builder", "the veteran").
- Private stories stay private. A story that reached the Sovereign privately (a friend's, a client's, a collaborator's) may travel as a de-identified pattern. A private detail travels only with that person's explicit consent.
- Plain-reader question, on every outward draft and every new name or label: would a stranger understand it without the ecosystem? Vocabulary that only makes sense inside the vault gets replaced with the plain word before the draft or label reaches the Sovereign. On high-reach pieces (pages, articles, product names) a cold read runs too: a fresh reader with no ecosystem context reads the piece and says what lands and what does not.
- Public copy that makes a claim bound to a time or a season is worth a second look, since the claim will not last. This is a consideration held with discernment, not an enforced rule.
- Before the vault's own week becomes outward copy, sort each item into one of three classes. Public as it stands (already published or already publicly linked to the Sovereign). Adapt first (private work, sessions, advisory questions and the month's builds feed copy as patterns, never as records). Never outward (names of clients or collaborators not publicly linked, money figures, health detail, keys and costs, internal scaffolding). The drafter sorts each item and flags every adapt-first item in the draft. The Sovereign rules those at the approval gate. An item that fits no class stays out until they rule it.
